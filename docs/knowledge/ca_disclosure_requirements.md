# California Real Estate Disclosure Requirements — Summary

> General educational summary for grounding assistant answers about
> California residential transactions. **Not legal advice.** Requirements
> change and have exceptions (e.g., certain probate, trustee, and REO
> sales); a party should consult a licensed California real estate broker
> or attorney for a specific transaction.

## Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS)

California Civil Code requires most sellers of residential property (1–4
units) to complete a Transfer Disclosure Statement, disclosing known
material facts about the property's condition — structural issues, roof
condition, past repairs, known defects in systems (plumbing, electrical,
HVAC), and other material facts that could affect value or desirability.
Delivered to the buyer as early as possible in the transaction, typically
before or shortly after offer acceptance.

## Natural Hazard Disclosure (NHD) Statement

Sellers must disclose whether the property lies within any of several
state-mapped natural hazard zones: flood zones, fire hazard severity zones,
earthquake fault zones, and seismic hazard zones. Usually prepared by a
third-party NHD report provider and delivered alongside the TDS.

## Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ)

A supplemental, more detailed questionnaire (commonly a C.A.R. form used
alongside the TDS) covering additional items: permits, neighborhood
nuisances, insurance claims history, deaths on the property, and other
disclosures the statutory TDS form doesn't itemize directly.

## Agency Relationship Disclosure

California requires real estate licensees to disclose which party (buyer,
seller, or both, in a dual agency) they represent, in writing, before the
parties enter a binding agreement — so both sides understand whose
interests the agent is obligated to represent.

## Lead-Based Paint Disclosure

For homes built before 1978, federal law requires sellers to disclose known
lead-based paint hazards and provide the buyer an EPA-approved pamphlet,
plus a window (commonly 10 days) for the buyer to conduct a lead
inspection or risk assessment before becoming bound to the contract.

## Megan's Law Database Disclosure

Purchase contracts must include a standard notice informing buyers that
information about registered sex offenders is available via the public
Megan's Law database, rather than requiring sellers to check and disclose
it themselves.

## Mello-Roos / HOA Disclosures

If a property is subject to a Mello-Roos special tax district or a
homeowners association, the seller must disclose the assessment amount and
provide HOA governing documents, financials, and any known special
assessments — relevant to the `AssociationFee` and `AssociationAmenities`
fields tracked in `rets_property` and `california_sold`.

## Water Heater & Smoke/CO Detector Compliance

Sellers typically must certify that water heaters are braced/strapped
against seismic movement and that the property has compliant smoke and
carbon monoxide detectors, per state building and fire code requirements.

## Timing & Buyer's Right to Cancel

Required disclosures are generally due to the buyer as soon as
practicable, often within days of offer acceptance. If disclosures are
delivered after the buyer has already signed the offer, the buyer
typically gets a statutory right to cancel the contract — 3 days if
disclosures are delivered in person, or 5 days if delivered by mail —
without penalty.
